	* Number 1 tip  
		* Cera: TAKE THE BREAKS. Get up and walk away from the computer and drink water and eat. Don't be tempted to use the breaks to do other work or catch up on something; you will be able to catch up on everything later \+1  
		* Scott: You get better the more you teach and don't expect to be perfect from the start. Teaching is also a learning experience, and you are never teaching alone, but teaching within a group which includes your co-instructor and the learners. \+1  
		* Jesse: Learn from your co-trainer. Have fun getting to spend a large chunk of teaching teaching with another trainer.\+1  
		* Trisha: The learners love to hear your experiences teaching and the experiences of other learners. Even if they seem shy, ask learners to speak out loud about their responses in the etherpad.  
		* Sarah: Bring your experience in teaching the techincal workshops into the training as expamples.  I think this is one of the things we can add to the curriculum.  Specific examples of how things have run in workshops or how you have used the teaching techniques/tools mentioned and how they worked in the class room.  
		* Sarah's other tip (said in meeting as well): I remember I feeling pressure to be "the perfect instructor" when first starting teaching about teaching.  I found it helpful to think of it as we are also modeling improving our instruction as well. If we correct ourselves or talk about what we are working on in our own teaching, that helps emphasize the importance of continuing to work on our teaching practice.

* Topic of the month: Meet the new Instructor trainers  
	* Existing Instructor Trainers: Share your Top Tip here:   
	* Cera: Take the breaks! Don't skip them!   
	* Figure out a system of organization of resources that works for you because there's a lot!  
	* pre-set up breakout rooms and allocate participants (plus record who was where)  
	* use the etherpad to list when people are coming back from a breakout room exercise before opening the rooms  
	* Refer to times by the minute and not the hour because people are at all different kinds of time zones  
		* I keep a local copy of the timing spreadsheet and change it to my time zone (for my reference)  
	* Use the training as a way to have open conversations and learn something too  
	* I read through my assigned lessons that I'll be leading a few days in advance - and then I make a brief bullet list of content timings in my local time (e.g. 12:00-12:10: Intro) - this helps me keep track if I'm running over /under time and either ramble on longer or shorter for exercises discussions, etc. I do this for my in-person classroom teaching too, it is great!  
	* I always prepare/review all the sections I'm teaching, but I pick one episode or section of an episode that I want to really focus on improving  
	* Check the time zone!

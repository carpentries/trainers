* Topic of the month: Instructor training Reading Club: Building Skill With Practice (https://carpentries.github.io/instructor-training/instructor/02-practice-learning.html)
	* Goal is not to get bogged down with specific edits to episodes until the end of our discussion - should focus on structure of episode
	* How do you manage the problem of getting through this much content without losing your instructor trainees’ motivation this early in the training?
		* Slides can/cannot help. Depends on the instructor and learner in question. Sometimes slides are helpful, but they can also distract. Visuals are usually key!
			* I think the slides help in this case to reduce the heavy text and focus on the concepts
		* This section has a nice sequencing in terms of walking through the visuals and being very clear how things fit together
		* Delegate/share content with a co-instructor whose teaching style is more calm and clear / good at lecturing. I prefer a more conversational approach but I always seem to be paired with someone who's good at introducing concepts thoughtfully, so I often let them teach this section.
		* I personally find this to be one of the most interesting episodes, so I actually haven't seen much trouble with keeping people's focus (perhaps surprisingly!). When I took the training, I really enjoyed this episode and was quite focused because my energy had not yet waned. [I also think that the exercises in this one are really interesting]
		* I try to maintain the enthusiasm we bring to the welcome/intro. I also like to be up front that we have set an expectation that the workshop will be very participatory but... the learners need to bear with us so we can go into some background.
		* I find this one has plenty of pictures to talk to and being very early on people are very interested but it often does feel like a concept dump
		* People often talk about the importance of these themes at the end of the training.
		* Difficulty of skipping parts because they come together.
		* Throw the learners in the deep end with the concept map activity. It is the first real activity after a lot of lecturing.
		* The analogy concept map can be tough - what is the value add for having them think through the analogy as versus a concept map of something they would teach. Struggling with this change.
		* Learners having difficulty coming up with analogies and then concept maps.
	* What are strategies that you have used to introduce concept maps?
		* Knowing what type of group of learners we have: chatty vs. quieter. In a more quiet group, will sometimes do a demonstration.
		* Emphasize that this is a preparation tool, not necessarily something you use during teaching or will make learners do in a workshop.
		* Plenty of learners want to know _exactly_ HOW TO CONCEPT MAP.
			* I wonder if one helpful thing would be to clearly state that “there is not one single correct way”. When I first learned concept maps it felt like I was supposed to “get it”, and being freed from the idea of a single right answer was helpful
		* I am curious to hear from others if concept maps is something learners actually put into practice after training?
			* they used to have a tie in with the lesson creation episode that was cut ages ago
		* Would this be an opportunity to do the activity together or to model doing a concept map?
			* Or could be a faded example instead of a blank canvas.
		* Difficulty can be part of the point. We are talking about teaching to novices, and the struggle people have is the struggle of a novice on concept maps.
			* Instructor note: Part of the point is that concept maps can be difficult and that we are novices of at this, just like learners. We should add this as a note in the lesson! I have never thought this way about that section before.
	* How do you handle the different experience levels of trainees when discussing formative vs summative assessment?
		* First episode where you get to see the experience of the trainees.
		* Learning on people witrh more experience contribute more to the discussion.
		* This section coming at the end episode can be a place that gets condensed.
		* Expanding or contracting based on experience of trainees.
		* Sometimes more experienced learners with no formal training on pedagogy struggle with this concept
* Space for reflection after discussing Building Skill With Practice:
	* Good to have the time for a deep dive together into a complex episode.
	* I found it really useful to talk through a lesson like this! Helps me prepare.

	* Talking about this helped me reflect on my own practices and teaching I've done, and very happy to share and discuss experiences with newer Trainers (also to remember why I really appreciate our community of practice!)
